Weyerhaeuser Reports Third Quarter Results

 

•
Generated net earnings of $61 million, or $0.08 per diluted share
•
Achieved Adjusted EBITDA of $248 million, a 14 percent increase compared with third quarter 2025
•
Advanced divestiture of non-core timberlands in Arkansas, with closing expected in the fourth quarter
•
Commencing construction on first biocarbon facility in McComb, Mississippi; site selection in progress for additional locations
•
Monetized approximately 340,000 forest carbon credits year-to-date through third quarter; on pace for highest annual sales volume since program’s inception
•
Commenced construction on two additional solar sites; bringing portfolio to two operational sites and five under construction
•
Signed geothermal energy exploration agreement covering approximately 145,000 acres in Oregon and Washington

 

SEATTLE, October 29, 2026 – Weyerhaeuser Company (NYSE: WY) today reported third quarter net earnings of $61 million, or 8 cents per diluted share, on net sales of $1.9 billion. This compares with net earnings of $80 million, or 11 cents per diluted share, on net sales of $1.7 billion for the same period last year and net earnings of $162 million for second quarter 2026. There were no special items in third quarter 2026. Net earnings before special items was $40 million for the same period last year and $91 million for second quarter 2026. Adjusted EBITDA for third quarter 2026 was $248 million, compared with $217 million for the same period last year and $310 million for second quarter 2026.

In October, Weyerhaeuser entered into an agreement to divest approximately 81,000 acres of non-core timberlands in Arkansas for $146 million. The transaction is expected to close in the fourth quarter and is subject to customary closing conditions. Consistent with the company’s long-standing approach to active portfolio management, Weyerhaeuser will continue to evaluate opportunities to optimize the return profile of its timberlands portfolio, while also creating additional flexibility for broader capital allocation and growth priorities as market conditions and opportunities evolve.

“Our teams delivered solid operating performance in the third quarter against an increasingly challenging macroeconomic backdrop,” said Devin W. Stockfish, president and chief executive officer. “In addition, we continued to enhance the value of our timberlands portfolio and made meaningful progress advancing several strategic growth initiatives. Looking ahead, we remain confident in the longer-term demand fundamentals that support growth for our businesses and are well positioned to navigate the current environment given the strength of our portfolio, balance sheet and flexible capital allocation framework. We remain intently focused on driving operational excellence, serving our customers and executing our strategy to create superior long-term value for our shareholders.”

Q3 2026 Performance – In the West, fee harvest volumes and forestry and road costs were comparable to the second quarter. Sales volumes were lower primarily due to fiber logs and the timing of export vessels, and per unit log and haul costs were also lower due to fewer export shipments. Q3 2026 Performance – For lumber, delivered sales realizations were slightly higher compared with the second quarter average. Sales volumes were moderately higher and log and unit manufacturing costs were slightly higher. For oriented strand board, sales realizations were 2 percent lower compared with the second quarter average. Sales volumes were slightly lower, fiber costs were comparable, and unit manufacturing costs were moderately higher due to more extensive planned annual maintenance and elevated resin costs. For engineered wood products, sales volumes were slightly higher for most products compared to the second quarter. Raw material and unit manufacturing costs were also slightly higher. Sales realizations were lower overall, partially driven by a mix shift within solid section products, but were higher for plywood. For distribution, results were lower than the second quarter, primarily due to decreased sales volumes and margins on specialty products.

Q4 2026 Outlook – Weyerhaeuser anticipates fourth quarter earnings before special items and Adjusted EBITDA will be moderately lower than the third quarter, excluding the effect of changes in average sales realizations for lumber and oriented strand board. For lumber, the company expects slightly lower sales volumes and slightly higher log and unit manufacturing costs. For oriented strand board, the company anticipates slightly higher sales volumes and fiber costs and slightly lower unit manufacturing costs. For engineered wood products, the company expects slightly higher sales realizations, slightly lower sales volumes for most products and moderately higher raw material costs. For distribution, the company anticipates slightly lower results compared to the third quarter.

ABOUT WEYERHAEUSER

Weyerhaeuser Company, one of the world's largest private owners of timberlands, began operations in 1900 and today owns or controls more than 10 million acres of timberlands in the U.S., as well as additional public timberlands managed under long-term licenses in Canada. Weyerhaeuser has been a global leader in sustainability for more than a century and manages 100 percent of its timberlands on a fully sustainable basis in compliance with internationally recognized sustainable forestry standards. Weyerhaeuser is also one of the largest manufacturers of wood products in North America and operates additional business lines around product distribution, climate solutions, real estate, and energy and natural resources, among others. In 2025, the company generated $6.9 billion in net sales and employed approximately 9,500 people who serve customers worldwide. Operated as a real estate investment trust, Weyerhaeuser’s common stock trades on the New York Stock Exchange under the symbol WY. Learn more at www.weyerhaeuser.com.
